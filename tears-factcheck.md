# Why Do Humans Cry?: fact check

Checked Nov 6 2026 by web search (abstracts, repository pages and review papers; one full-text PDF host was blocked in the cloud session). The owner's draft was mostly right; corrections are marked **FIXED**.

| Claim in draft | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Three kinds of tears: basal, reflex, emotional | Textbook-level; standard classification. | |
| Blinking spreads the tear film; thousands of blinks a day | Textbook-level (about 15-20 blinks a minute while awake). | |
| Reflex tears from dust, smoke, wind, onion; watering when yawning or laughing hard | Textbook-level. | |
| Frey 1981: emotional tears about 24% more protein than onion tears | **FIXED wording.** Frey, DeSota-Johnson, Hoffman & McCall, "Effect of stimulus on the chemical composition of human tears", *American Journal of Ophthalmology* 92(4):559-567 (1981). Abstract: "The protein concentration of emotional tears from women exceeded that of irritant-induced tears by 24%." The comparison was in women. Script now says "in women's tears" and drops the unverified quote "even Frey said scientists don't really know"; it says instead that nobody is sure what the difference means. | EPA HERO record of the paper |
| Tears drain at the inner corner into a channel and into the nose; crying hard makes the nose run | Textbook-level (puncta, canaliculi, lacrimal sac, nasolacrimal duct). | |
| Newborns make some tears from birth but not enough to spill; visible tears usually at 1-3 months | Confirmed by pediatricians quoted in parenting press (Children's Health, Dallas, via The Bump: "between 1 and 3 months"; Vinmec: 1 to 3 months). No AAP page found. Ranges elsewhere: 2 weeks to 3 months. Script: "for the first weeks" (not "two weeks"), "usually somewhere between one and three months". | The Bump; Vinmec |
| 7,000+ people, 41 countries, photos with digitally added tears, more willingness to support | Confirmed. Zickfeld et al., "Tears evoke the intention to offer social support: A systematic investigation of the interpersonal effects of emotional crying across 41 countries", *Journal of Experimental Social Psychology* 95:104137 (2021), registered report. 7,007 participants, 41 countries, effect d = 0.49. | ScienceDirect; Tilburg repository |
| 1980s surveys: 85% of women, 73% of men felt better after crying | **FIXED wording.** Frey et al. (1983), "Crying behavior in the human adult": 85% of women and 73% of men said they *generally* felt better after crying (a trait question). In the same study's diary of actual episodes, mood improved after only about 40% of them. Script: "In one study from the early 1980s, when people were asked how they usually feel after crying..." | Bylsma et al. 2011 (*J Research in Personality*), describing Frey 1983 |
| 5,000+ students, 35 countries, most recent cry: most better, a third no change, one in ten worse | **FIXED numbers.** Bylsma, Vingerhoets & Rottenberg, "When is crying cathartic? An international study", *Journal of Social and Clinical Psychology* 27(10):1165-1187 (2008): 5,096 students (2,181 men, 2,915 women) in 35 countries. Mood after the most recent cry: 51.4% better, 38.3% the same, 9.7% worse (figures as cited in a Hrčak review; the original PDF host was blocked). Script: "about half felt better, close to four in ten felt no change, and about one in ten felt worse." | USF Digital Commons abstract; Hrčak review "Crying and Health" |
| Social support predicted feeling better; shame or negative responses predicted feeling worse | Confirmed, same 2008 paper (support, resolution, positive events -> better; negative responses, embarrassment or shame, witnessing others' suffering -> worse). | same |
| Holding back tears not proven harmful | Consistent with the reviews (Rottenberg, Bylsma & Vingerhoets 2008, "Is crying beneficial?", *Current Directions in Psychological Science* 17:400-404: survey and lab evidence disagree; health claims not established). Framed as "science hasn't proven that". | SAGE |

## Wording fix (not a fact)
S03 "even reading this sentence would get uncomfortable" -> "and even just keeping them open would get uncomfortable" (it is a voiceover).

## Do NOT say
"Crying flushes toxins/stress hormones out" (not in the 1981 paper); "emotional tears are chemically proven different" (one old study, women only); "most people feel better after crying" without the caveat; "holding tears in makes you ill"; "babies can't make tears".
