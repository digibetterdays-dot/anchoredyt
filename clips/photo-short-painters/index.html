“The refuge of every would-be painter…”
Charles Baudelaire, Salon of 1859
For painters,
the timing could
hardly have been
worse.
Think about who
earned a living
from faces.
Portrait painters.
Miniature painters.
Silhouette cutters.
Now a machine
could take a
likeness in minutes,
at a fraction
of the price,
and with a
precision no hand
could match.
For portrait painters,
this was a
new competitor.
People could have
a photograph made
instead of commissioning
a painting.
Some artists saw
an opportunity.
Others saw the
end of their
trade.
The poet and
critic Charles Baudelaire
was furious.
Photography,
he wrote,
was "the refuge
of every would-be
painter,
every painter too
ill-endowed or too
lazy to complete
his studies."
