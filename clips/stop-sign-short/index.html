Illustration
Stop signs weren't always red.
Or eight-sided.
Early stop signs were yellow.
Fade-proof red paint
wasn't available yet.
In the 1920s, highway officials
standardized road signs.
And gave the stop sign
a shape no other sign used.
A unique shape tells you what
a sign says before you read it.
Even at night, when early
signs weren't reflective.
A shape designed to be unmistakable,
about a century ago.
