All this relaxing
has a side
effect you may
have heard from
across the room.
As you sink
deeper into sleep,
the muscles of
your throat,
tongue and soft
palate relax.
Air rushing past
that loose tissue
makes it vibrate,
and that's a
snore.
Sleeping on your
back,
or after alcohol,
can make the
airway narrower and
the snore louder.
But loud snoring
with pauses in
breathing can be
a sign of
sleep apnea,
and that's worth
checking with a
doctor.
