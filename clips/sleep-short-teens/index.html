A teenager who
struggles to fall
asleep at nine
o'clock may not
simply be lazy
or undisciplined.
During puberty,
the biological timing
of sleep naturally
shifts later for
many young people.
The evening release
of melatonin tends
to come later,
and sleep pressure
builds more slowly.
The result?
A teenager may
feel wide awake
late at night,
and struggle to
wake up early
for school.
That creates a
conflict between the
teenage body clock
and the early
schedules of modern
life.
The American Academy
of Sleep Medicine
recommends that teenagers
aged 13 to
18 regularly get
8 to 10
hours of sleep.
So the next
time a teenager
says,
"I'm just not
sleepy yet," remember:
there may be
a genuine biological
reason.
