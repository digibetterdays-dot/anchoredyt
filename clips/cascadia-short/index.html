The “orphan tsunami”
Japan, January 1700
About magnitude 9
January 26, 1700, about 9 p.m.
More than 40
magnitude 8+ quakes in 10,000 years · NOAA
How do we
know it's happened?
Because of a
tsunami that struck
Japan with no
earthquake to explain
it.
In January of
the year 1700,
a wave about
six to ten
feet high hit
Japan's Honshu coast.
Nobody there felt
any shaking.
Japanese records called
it the "orphan
tsunami",
a wave with
no parent earthquake.
Researchers later connected
it to Cascadia,
and used those
records,
and how long
a wave takes
to cross the
Pacific,
to pin the
quake to the
night of January
26th,
at about nine
o'clock.
It's estimated at
about magnitude 9.
And it's not
a one-off.
According to NOAA,
geologic evidence shows
more than 40
earthquakes above magnitude
8 along this
zone over the
last 10,000 years.
