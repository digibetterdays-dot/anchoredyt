# What Happens If a Nuclear Bomb Goes Off?: fact check

Researched Nov 7 2026 by web search (FEMA, Ready.gov, CDC, HHS REMM, RERF and journal pages; ready.gov PDFs were blocked for direct fetch in the cloud session, their text came via search). Scenario throughout: **a single ~10-kiloton surface (ground-level) detonation**, the planning case in FEMA's *Planning Guidance for Response to a Nuclear Detonation* (3rd ed., 2022). Distances are for that case and "will vary with yield".

**Scope rules (owner):** effects and survival only; no current countries, leaders or targets; nothing about how weapons are built or used.

| Planning case: single ~10 kT device at ground level | Confirmed (FEMA 3rd ed. and REMM use a nominal 10 kT surface/ground burst). | FEMA Planning Guidance 3rd ed. (fema.gov PDF); REMM "Damage Zones" |
| Flash blindness without looking at it; reflected light enough; daylight seconds, up to ~2 min; longer at night | Confirmed. At night ... will last longer." | REMM "Nuclear Detonation: Weapons, Improvised Nuclear Devices" |
| Looking at the fireball can burn the retina, permanent, many miles away | Confirmed. Script says "many miles". | REMM; HHS/REMM Planning Guidance PDF |
| Inside the fireball everything vaporizes, drawn upward into the mushroom cloud | Confirmed (REMM "Mushroom Cloud"). | REMM |
| Severe burns and fires to about a mile (10 kT) | Confirmed. REMM/National Academies: "Severe thermal damage would extend out about a mile." | REMM "Damage Zones" |
| Blast wave arrives a few seconds to 30 s or more after the flash, by distance | Confirmed as range. American Red Cross: "it could take 30 seconds or more for the blast wave to hit" if some distance away. | Red Cross "Nuclear Explosion and Radiation Emergencies" |
| If outside: lie face down behind something solid, protect skin, stay down until blast/heat pass; in a vehicle stop and duck | Confirmed (Ready.gov wording via state EIN page; U.S. Air Force Be Ready). | Ready.gov; ein.az.gov; beready.af.mil |

| Moderate damage ~1 mile: blown-out interiors, overturned cars, fires, some collapses | Confirmed (REMM zone page). | REMM |
| Light damage ~3 miles; flying glass main injury; windows broken >10 miles away | Confirmed (REMM: "some windows may be broken over 10 miles away"; light damage ~3 mi). | REMM |

| Myth: everyone dies instantly | Supported: severe damage zone is ~0.5 mi; CDC: people even in the moderate damage zone and the fallout zone can survive; FEMA: sheltering may save hundreds of thousands. | CDC radiation myths (Myth 5); FEMA 72-hour guidance |
| Fallout = debris mixed with radioactive material drawn into the cloud, falls back | Confirmed (REMM "Fallout"). | REMM |
| Fallout near the blast within minutes; often 15+ minutes outside blast damage; "10 minutes or more" to find shelter | Confirmed. | Falls downwind in a plume; most dangerous 10-20 miles downwind; highest hazard first 4-6 hours | Confirmed (REMM; planning guidance). | REMM |
| Dangerous fallout often visible like fine sand or table salt; not seeing it doesn't mean safe | Confirmed. FEMA 3rd ed.: "often being the size of fine sand or table salt"; earlier eds.: lack of apparent fallout "should not be misrepresented to mean radiation isn't present". | FEMA |
| Car blocks little; basement much better; middle of large brick/concrete building or underground garage best | Confirmed. Cars PF 3 or less; basements ~PF 10 (Monmouth Co. NJ citing Buddemeier 2009, FEMA 2011); office building or underground garage best (Buddemeier, NYT 2010); Raleigh: brick or concrete best, away from outer walls and roof. | as listed |

FEMA PrepTalk (Brooke Buddemeier, LLNL): 10 kT in Washington DC, "280,000 casualties from fallout radiation" if no one shelters for 12 hours; facilitator slides: "Casualties from fallout are almost entirely preventable." Script does not name the city. | FEMA PrepTalk discussion guide and slides |
| 7-10 rule: 7 h -> 10%, ~2 days -> 1%, ~2 weeks -> 0.1% | Confirmed. Planning guidance / DHS quick reference: "For every sevenfold increase in time after detonation, there is a tenfold decrease in the radiation rate"; accurate within ~25% up to ~2 weeks; measured from detonation time. | HHS/REMM and NRC copies of Planning Guidance; DHS Quick Reference Guide |
| Stay inside at least 24 h unless told otherwise; turn off fans/AC that pull in outside air; pets inside | Confirmed (Ready.gov "Radiation Emergencies"). | Ready.gov; EPA |
| Remove outer clothing: up to 90%; bag it; shower with soap and shampoo, no conditioner; don't scrub | Confirmed (CDC self-decontamination page and infographic). | CDC |
| Sealed food and unspoiled fridge/freezer food safe; wipe packaging; bottled water best | Confirmed. Ready.gov flyer "Nuclear Detonation Safety: Food, Drinking Water and Medicine"; FEMA 2024 messaging guide: packaged foods "still safe to use after wiping". | Battery or hand-crank radio will work | Confirmed (Raleigh NC preparedness guide; FEMA messaging). | raleighnc.gov |
| Myth: one bomb makes the whole planet radioactive | Supported: fallout regional, downwind, decays fast (7-10 rule; CDC Myth 5; Utah: ~1% of initial level after two weeks). | CDC; Utah DEM |
| Radiation sickness: nausea/vomiting within hours; latent phase days to weeks; bone marrow damage -> infection and bleeding; higher dose = sooner and worse | Confirmed (CDC ARS physician brochure; Medical management of ARS review, PMC3863169; Borden Institute text). | CDC; PMC |
| Center blocked by rubble; infrastructure beyond ~5 miles only nominally affected | Confirmed. REMM: rubble "impassable in the SD zone"; "Infrastructure more than five miles away from ground zero are likely to be only nominally affected by blast and EMP of a 10kT device". | REMM |
| >100,000 Hiroshima/Nagasaki survivors followed for 70+ years | Confirmed. RERF Life Span Study: 105,444 people in the 1958-2009 incidence study; follow-up since 1950 ("Eight decades of research", PMC12558143). | RERF / Radiation Research |
| About 1 in 10 solid cancers among survivors linked to radiation | Confirmed. Grant et al., *Radiation Research* 2017: 992 of 22,538 first primary solid cancers associated with radiation; attributable fraction ~10% (11% in Preston 2007). | PMC10320812 |

| Hiroshima today home to more than a million people | Confirmed: 1,172,423 (2025 census, preliminary). | Statistics Bureau of Japan via citypopulation.de |

## Deliberately NOT in the script
Yields of real weapons or arsenals; any country, leader, target or scenario of who attacks whom; how weapons work or are delivered; casualty totals for real cities; nuclear-winter death tolls (contested); "duck and cover" as a cure-all; potassium iodide (not part of the federal first-hour message; would need its own check); "fallout is harmless if you can't see it".

## Do NOT say
"Everyone within X miles dies"; "the planet becomes uninhabitable" from one bomb; "radiation lasts forever"; "a car will protect you"; any yield or distance as exact (they are for a 10 kT planning case and vary).
