# Why Do We Sleep?: fact check

Checked Nov 5 2026 by web search (PubMed itself is blocked in the cloud session; abstracts came via ScienceDirect, Kent repository, journal pages and search summaries). The owner's draft was already careful; corrections are marked **FIXED**.

| Claim in script | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2024 meta-analysis: 39 reports, 1,234 participants; sleep restriction linked to poorer memory formation | **Confirmed.** Crowley, Alderman, Javadi & Tamminen, "A systematic and meta-analytic review of the impact of sleep restriction on memory formation", *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* 167:105929 (Dec 2024), PMID 39427809. Restriction to 3-6.5 h vs 7-11 h; small effect (g = 0.29); impairs encoding and consolidation; slow-wave restriction hurts most. | ScienceDirect abstract; Kent repository PDF |
| Sleep cycles "80-100 minutes", 4-6 per night | **FIXED to "about 90 minutes".** NIH StatPearls: ~90 min average (90-110), 4-6 cycles; first cycle can be shorter (70-100). | StatPearls "Physiology, Sleep Stages" |
| First REM about 90 min after sleep onset | Confirmed (StatPearls; Cleveland Clinic via Texas Health). | |
| DEC2 mutation, 2009 *Science*, "a family" | **FIXED to "a mother and daughter".** He et al., "The transcriptional repressor DEC2 regulates sleep length in mammals", *Science* 325:866-870 (2009): heterozygous P385R mutation in a mother and daughter (about 6.25 h vs 8.06 h habitual sleep); transgenic mice slept less. Only two human carriers. | OMIM 612975; UCSF; Science |
| Newborns 14-17 h | **FIXED: attributed to the National Sleep Foundation** (AASM makes no recommendation under 4 months). NSF 2015, reaffirmed by a 10-year review in 2026. | Hirshkowitz et al. 2015, *Sleep Health*; NSF |
| Teens 13-18: 8-10 h (AASM) | Confirmed. | AASM Teen Sleep Duration Health Advisory |
| Adults "18-60 at least 7 h" | **Kept general:** "sleep experts recommend that most adults regularly get at least seven" (AASM/SRS: adults 7 h or more). | AASM; sleepeducation.org |
| Teen sleep phase delay; melatonin later; sleep pressure builds more slowly; Carskadon | Confirmed. Crowley, Acebo & Carskadon (2007), *Sleep Medicine* 8:602-612; Jenni, Achermann & Carskadon (2005), *Sleep*; Crowley et al. (2018) "perfect storm" update. Script wording: "Researchers including Mary Carskadon have studied these developmental changes". | |
| Fjell & Walhovd 2025 review: no consensus on one fundamental function | Confirmed. "Sleep Patterns and Human Brain Health", *The Neuroscientist* (2025), doi 10.1177/10738584241309850: "lacking consensus regarding its fundamental underlying function"; groups ecological/evolutionary, restoration and waste-removal, cognitive theories. | SAGE |

| Snoring: throat, tongue and soft-palate muscles relax going into deeper sleep; air past loose tissue vibrates; worse on your back, after alcohol; loud snoring with pauses can signal sleep apnea | Confirmed (Mayo Clinic "Snoring: symptoms and causes"). No prevalence figure used: the only one found came from a patent filing. | Mayo Clinic |
| Sleep deprivation impairs suppression of unwanted memories; suppressed thoughts more likely to return | Confirmed. Harrington, Ashton, Sankarasubramanian, Anderson & Cairney, "Losing Control: Sleep Deprivation Impairs the Suppression of Unwanted Thoughts", *Clinical Psychological Science* (online Oct 2020; 2021 issue), PMC7820573. One night of total sleep deprivation vs sleep; think/no-think task with negative and neutral scenes. fMRI follow-up in *PNAS* (doi 10.1073/pnas.2400743122). The "nearly 50%" figure is from press coverage, not used. | PMC; PNAS |

## Do NOT say
"Melatonin switches your brain off"; "your brain is washed clean every night"; "you can train yourself to need less sleep"; a single reason why we sleep; "a family with the DEC2 gene"; that the meta-analysis found a large effect.
